# Ganymede settlement contracts

These contracts separate the fund's off-chain asset custody and execution from its on-chain share and disclosure records.

- `GanymedeFundShare.sol` is a six-decimal, permissioned fund-share ledger. Only allowlisted investors can hold or transfer shares. Subscription and redemption settlement identifiers are one-time and idempotent.
- `GanymedeNavRegistry.sol` publishes monotonic NAV snapshots and rebalance evidence hashes.
- `GanymedeBasketFund.sol` is the USTX share token on X Layer Testnet. `invest(dollars, minShares)` takes demo dollars and issues shares at the latest NAV that `GanymedeNavRegistry` holds for `us-tech-x`; `redeem(shares, minDollars)` burns shares and pays demo dollars at that NAV. Orders need a record at most an hour old and at least $10, round down both ways, and revert past the caller's minimum. It holds no assets, no key can issue shares directly, and `investorCount()` counts holders on chain. The administrator can only pause.
- `GanymedeDemoDollar.sol` (dUSD) is a no-value demo dollar: anyone can `claim()` 10,000 once a day, and only the fund contract can mint more, to pay redemptions.
- `GanymedeNavFeed.sol` exposes one product's NAV from `GanymedeNavRegistry` through the `AggregatorV3Interface` that Chainlink price feeds use (`latestRoundData`, `getRoundData`, `decimals`, `description`, `version`, plus the older `latestAnswer`, `latestTimestamp` and `latestRound`), so apps that already read Chainlink prices can read USTX without custom code. Answers have 8 decimals (the registry's 6-decimal NAV × 100). The round ID, `startedAt` and `updatedAt` are the record's effective time, so a staleness check on `updatedAt` measures the age of the prices. Only the latest round is on chain; any other round ID reverts with `NoDataPresent`. It has no owner and nothing to configure.
- `GanymedeUstxPool.sol` is the secondary market: a constant-product pool of USTX and dUSD with Uniswap V2 arithmetic, a 0.3% fee to liquidity providers, slippage minimums and deadlines. Its price moves only with trades, so it drifts from the NAV as the NAV moves; `premiumBps()` reports the gap against the fund's `currentNav()`.
- `GanymedeNavArbitrage.sol` closes that gap in one transaction, the way ETF creation and redemption keep a fund near its NAV: below the NAV it buys USTX in the pool and redeems it at the fund (`buyAndRedeem`); above it, it invests at the fund and sells the new USTX in the pool (`investAndSell`). It reverts unless the caller gets back at least the input plus `minProfit`, and `quote()` returns the size that captures most of the gap. It has no owner and holds nothing between transactions.
- `GanymedeLendingMarket.sol` lends dUSD against USTX. Lenders `supply` dUSD and earn the interest borrowers pay, on a jump-rate curve (2% a year plus 10% × utilization up to 80%, then 200% × the excess); 10% of the interest stays in the market as reserves. Borrowers post USTX with `supplyCollateral` and `borrow` up to 50% of its value at the fund's `currentNav()`, so a stale NAV stops new loans. Once a loan's debt passes 65% of that value, anyone can `liquidate` up to half of it and receive USTX worth 8% more at NAV, which the fund redeems at NAV. The market starts paused, and pausing never blocks repaying, withdrawing or liquidating. It is live on X Layer Testnet at `0xae2f54ae3d0370295de18510d56de92afb8843c7`, activated on 25 September with the user's approval, and the USTX page's Borrow section uses it.

- `GanymedeBasketVault.sol` is an in-kind alternative to that custody path, run only on a fork and never deployed. Shares are created only by delivering the constituents and redeemed for them, as an ETF's authorized participants deliver and receive the underlying shares. The first creation takes a fixed quantity of each constituent per share, set at deployment; after that every share is an equal claim on everything the vault holds, so `create(shares)` delivers each token in proportion to the holdings and `redeem(shares)` pays each out in proportion. It prices nothing and has no owner. xStocks keep balances as shares times a multiplier: a transfer can arrive a base unit short, and a dividend, split or fee changes every balance, which proportional accounting passes to the shares. Creation takes the proportional amount rounded up plus `ROUNDING_ALLOWANCE` (4 base units) and reverts if any token arrives short of the proportional amount, so a fee-on-transfer token cannot dilute holders; redemption pays the proportional amount rounded down less the allowance. The first creation must be at least one whole share, and `create` takes the most of each token the caller will deliver, so a dust share and a donation cannot make shares expensive. `npm run fork:vault` in `onchain/` runs it against the real xStocks on a fork of X Layer mainnet.
- `GanymedeRwaLiquidityHook.sol` is a Uniswap v4 hook that runs a real-world-asset/dollar pool around the asset's NAV (USTX/dUSD, priced by `GanymedeNavFeed`) and is the vault of its liquidity providers. It opens its one pool at the NAV in its constructor and refuses other pools and any liquidity it does not add itself, so it holds all of the pool's liquidity and issues ERC-20 shares (`USTX-V4LP`) for it: the first deposit mints its value at the NAV and locks 1,000 shares; later deposits are priced forward, like fund subscriptions, waiting at the holdings' ratio until the first NAV record published after them converts them into shares at that NAV (claimable after, cancellable before; a deposit applies any record the pool has not yet re-pegged to first), so they earn nothing from trades made before their liquidity is in the pool; withdrawals receive the shares' part of everything held, rounded down, fees included, with any NAV. The liquidity sits in a base range about 2% either side of the NAV and a one-sided range for what the base range cannot use, on the side where that token sells. At each new NAV record the hook takes its liquidity out, converts the waiting deposits, moves the empty pool to the NAV (a swap that exchanges nothing) and puts everything back, so the price follows the NAV without arbitrage against the providers. The fee is 0.30% with a fresh NAV, rising to 1.00% at an hour; swaps stop past an hour or on a record dated more than a minute after its block, and may not leave the price more than about 5% from the NAV. It has no owner and deploys through CREATE2 at an address carrying its permissions. `GanymedeV4Router.sol` swaps on it with exact input or output, limits, a deadline and `eth_call` quotes. Neither is deployed: `npm run fork:v4` runs them against the live testnet fund on a fork, next to Uniswap's own PoolManager build (the code Uniswap runs on X Layer mainnet), and `docs/UNISWAP_V4_LIQUIDITY.md` records the design and the run.
- `testing/ForkPoolBuyer.sol` (buys an xStock through its Uniswap V3 pool and unwraps it), `testing/FeeOnTransferToken.sol` and `testing/MultiplierToken.sol` (balances as shares times a multiplier, like the xStocks) exist only for those tests and are never deployed.

The fund, demo-dollar, lending and Uniswap v4 liquidity contracts are for testnet demonstration with no value; they are not the production settlement path described below.

Production deployment requirements:

1. Independent administrator, issuer, transfer-agent and publisher multisigs.
2. KYC/AML before allowlisting an investor (on GIWA, also an Upbit Korea Dojang Verified Address check).
3. External custody and cash settlement confirmation before minting or burning shares.
4. Contract audit, deployment rehearsal, monitoring and emergency runbook.
5. A licensed fund, transfer agent, custodian and approved offering documents.

The backend never stores an EVM private key. It submits idempotent requests to a separately operated settlement relayer (`relayer/`) configured through hosted secrets. The contracts are plain EVM and deploy unchanged to X Layer (default) or GIWA Sepolia.
